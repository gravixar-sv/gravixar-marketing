## How I picked the tool

I had used HubSpot for two years on an NDIS project for an Australian client. That gave me a practical benchmark before I compared anything: the tool has to fit the workflow, not the other way around.

monday.com won because the interface clicked, and it supported the written procedures, automations and dashboards the rollout was built on.

## Change management was the real work

The biggest pushback came from account managers and project managers. monday.com asked for clearer briefs, consistent statuses, and disciplined handoffs. For the first time, work became visible, including the delays and the missing inputs.

The teams doing the work adopted it faster, because they got clarity, a record of what they delivered, and less guessing.

How I handled the pushback:

- Wrote the procedures early and kept improving them
- Trained people by role (account and project managers vs. the teams doing the work)
- Used their feedback to refine statuses and templates
- Kept ownership with Ops at first, then handed it to the teams

## Key learnings I'd carry into the next rollout

- Adoption beats features. Pick what teams will actually use.
- Start with clarity: roles, statuses, and procedures before automations.
- Roll out in phases and prove the value with one team first.
- Dashboards only work if the data underneath is clean.
- Tools don't create accountability. Ownership does.

## What's next

AI is now being added to briefs, updates, and content work, to cut manual steps without taking ownership away from people.

If I set this up again for a similar agency, I'd still pick monday.com for this kind of team. I'd just move faster, with the lessons above.
