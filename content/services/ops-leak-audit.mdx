The Ops Leak Audit is for agencies, and for accounting and law practices, in the UK and the UAE that want to know where their team's hours go before they buy or build anything. It's $3,500 fixed for one team and the workflows that cost it the most, about £2,600 or AED 12,850 at today's exchange rates, and you get a written report and a call to walk you through it about two weeks after kickoff. I run it remotely from my office in Pakistan. The closest named proof is my work for Broomstick Creative, an agency working across the UAE and Pakistan, where I compared tools against how the team actually worked before rolling out monday.com, and the team grew on it from 10 people to more than 50.

Most operations problems get counted in the wrong unit. A tool costs $25 a seat, so the tool looks cheap. The hours spent around it never reach an invoice.

Those hours are the leak. The Friday afternoon a project manager spends matching up two systems that disagree. The approval chased by email because the portal can't handle it. The client status update three people put together by hand. Nothing breaks, and you pay for all of it every week, at salary rates.

## What I do

1. **Talk to the people who do the work,** not just whoever picked the tools. The person patching a spreadsheet on Friday knows where the time goes, and usually nobody has asked them.
2. **List every tool.** Every tool in the workflow, the seats, the monthly cost, and what each one is really used for. That last column is where the surprises sit.
3. **Watch the expensive workflows run.** A walkthrough or a screen recording of the few workflows that eat the most time, so the count comes from what happens rather than from what the process document says happens.
4. **Count the leak.** Hours a week per workflow, each number written next to the interview or recording it came from, so you can check every one yourself.
5. **Price the fix.** What to change, what to keep, and a fixed price for anything worth building.

## What you receive

A written report and a call to walk you through it, about two weeks after kickoff. The report is yours either way. If the fix is a build, the report already includes its fixed price, so the next conversation starts from a number instead of another round of scoping calls. You know what the answer costs before you ask the question.

## What the report looks like

Page one is the answer: how many hours your team loses each week, what they cost, and what I would do about them, including when the answer is to keep the tool you have. After that come the count by workflow, every tool with its seats and monthly cost, and the priced fix.

## What it asks of your team

About four hours of your team's time, spread over the two weeks:

- A 45-minute kickoff with you, to agree the team and the workflows that matter most.
- Thirty minutes each with two to four of the people who do the work.
- A screen recording of each costly workflow, made while the work happens, so it takes no extra time.
- A 60-minute readout at the end, with anyone you want in the room.

All of it works remotely, so it runs the same for a team in the UK or the UAE.

## What one team means

One group of people who share a workflow and the tools it runs on, usually 5 to 25 people: the delivery team, client services, or a practice's accounts team. If the leak crosses into a second team, I say so at the kickoff, and the second team is priced as a second audit.

## The terms, plainly

- **Price.** $3,500 fixed, invoiced in US dollars. That is about £2,600 or AED 12,850 at today's exchange rates.
- **Payment.** Half when you book the start date, half after the readout. If the readout was not useful to you, the second half is not due.
- **Credit.** If you start a build with me within 90 days of the readout, the full $3,500 comes off its fixed price.
- **Confidentiality.** I sign your NDA before the kickoff, or send you mine. Recordings and documents are used for the audit and nothing else, and deleted when you ask or when the work ends.
