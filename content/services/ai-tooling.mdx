I build AI automation and AI agents for agencies and for accounting and law practices in the UK and the UAE, working remotely from my office in Pakistan, and nothing the AI drafts goes out or changes a record until a person approves it. It's priced once I see the scope, and a first build usually takes 2 to 4 weeks. The named example is Broomstick Hub, the agency portal I built for Broomstick Creative, an agency working across the UAE and Pakistan. There, AI picks the next intake question and drafts a brand brief from the prospect's website, and it never approves anything or messages a client on its own.

The plainest example runs on this site. Twice a week an AI drafts an article from what people are searching for and drops it in a folder for review, and nothing goes live until I approve it. I wrote up [the 41 days this blog went without a post](/blog/bottleneck-is-approval-not-generation) while four drafts sat waiting, because that wait is the real limit on this kind of system, and the approval step is the part I sell.

## What it usually looks like

- **A smarter enquiry form.** A new lead answers 4 to 7 questions that change based on their answers, and AI writes a brand brief from their website. Your team walks into the first call already knowing the basics.
- **A nightly watch.** Every night it checks your activity log for work that is stuck, sudden floods of signups, bursts of deleted records, and anything else unusual. You get an email when something looks off, and silence when everything is fine.
- **Posts that wait for you.** Drafts line up for review. You approve once, and it posts to LinkedIn, X, and your blog. A person always makes the call.
- **A sorted inbox.** New messages get sorted by urgency, topic, and intent, with a suggested owner. A person confirms.

If you already run systems and want AI working inside them, this is the job.

## For accounting and law practices

On [Beeline's credentialing platform](/work/beeline), every free-text box is checked for patient details before anything is saved, and every AI request carries a standing instruction to keep them out. That's the pattern I'd bring to a practice holding clients' tax or legal documents. AI drafts and sorts, and a person signs off on anything that leaves the firm. I haven't published a build for an accounting or law firm yet, so Beeline is the proof I can point to, and the [page for accounting and law practices](/for/accounting-and-law-firms) says what a portal for a firm would hold.
