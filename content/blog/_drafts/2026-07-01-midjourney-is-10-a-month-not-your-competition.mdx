## The Commodity Layer Has Already Collapsed

Midjourney runs about $10 a month on the basic plan. Runway is under $50 for most agency-level video volumes. Kling is in the same range. If you need raw generation capacity, you can have it for less than a lunch.

This is not a trend. It is done. The floor on AI generation pricing is effectively zero for most use cases, and it is not coming back up.

I want to be direct about what that means: if your value proposition to a client is "I use AI to make images faster and cheaper," you are selling a commodity that now costs $10 a month. That is a bad place to be.

But that framing is also wrong about what brand system work actually is.

## What a Midjourney Output Actually Is

A Midjourney output is a file. It might be a good file. It might be a great file. What it is not is a decision.

There is no record of why that composition was chosen over the three alternatives generated in the same batch. There is no log of which version was approved, by whom, and on what date. There is no token system ensuring the blue in that image matches the blue in the packaging, the hero banner, and the A+ detail page. There is no revision trail showing what the client asked to change between v2 and v4.

The file exists. The context around the file does not.

For a one-off social post, that is fine. For Amazon A+ content that is going to live on a product listing at scale, be updated quarterly, and need to pass brand review by a compliance team, the file is the least important part of the deliverable.

## The Gap Is Governance, Not Generation

Here is what a governed A+ content workflow actually contains:

A brief intake with locked brand parameters — hex values, approved typefaces, required copy claims, restricted imagery. Not stored in someone's head. Stored in a document that is versioned and referenced in every job.

A generation stage where multiple directions are explored against those parameters. This stage is where Midjourney, Flux, or whatever model makes sense gets used. Generation is a step, not the product.

An internal review checkpoint before anything goes to the client. This exists to catch brand violations, factual errors in copy overlays, and technical spec failures before they cost client trust. I wrote about this pattern before — removing this step to save time is how agencies train clients not to trust their first drafts.

A structured client approval interface. Not an email thread. Not a shared Drive folder with comments in the filename. A stage-gated review where feedback is captured against a specific version and the approval is recorded with a timestamp.

A revision log that shows what changed between each version, who requested it, and what was accepted. This is the document that protects you in a dispute and speeds up the next similar project.

A final delivery package with the approved asset, the brief, the approval record, and the usage spec. The client gets a system artifact, not just a PNG.

None of that is replaced by a $10 Midjourney subscription.

## Why I Think About This as Infrastructure

I have been running a client portal for agency work for four years. Early on, I treated deliverables as files. A job was done when the file was approved and sent.

The problem is that clients come back. They ask why the new version looks different from the one from eight months ago. They ask who approved the last change. They ask for the layered file from the previous round. Those questions are unanswerable if you ran the work as file transfers.

The system I run now stores the brief, the generation inputs, every reviewed version, the approval record, and the delivery package. When a client asks about a decision from eight months ago, I can answer in under two minutes. That is not a capability Midjourney provides. It is not a capability any generation tool provides. It is a capability the workflow infrastructure provides.

The generation cost inside that workflow is now nearly zero. The infrastructure is not.

## The Positioning Error to Avoid

The agencies that will feel the price collapse are the ones who positioned themselves as fast, cheap, AI-powered image production. That is a direct substitute for a $10 subscription, and clients will eventually notice.

The positioning that is not threatened is system operator. Someone who runs the governance layer, maintains the brand token logic, manages the approval workflow, and delivers a traceable artifact rather than a file.

I am not saying that positioning is automatically correct for every project or every client. Some clients genuinely need fast, disposable content and have no compliance requirements. Midjourney is the right answer for them, and I will tell them that.

But for clients running product lines with brand standards, approval obligations, and content that lives in commercial contexts for months or years, the generation cost is a line item inside a system. Selling them generation is selling them the wrong thing.

## What This Changes in Practice

If you are doing brand asset work right now, the audit question is simple: what do you deliver beyond the file?

If the answer is unclear, the price collapse in generation tools is a signal worth taking seriously. Not because the work is less valuable, but because the value needs to be visible in something that a $10 subscription cannot replicate.

Approval records, revision history, brand token documentation, and delivery governance are not glamorous. They are also not commodities. That distinction is worth building around.
