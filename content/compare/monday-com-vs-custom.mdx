## The honest version

I have rolled monday.com out for clients who stayed on it. I've also helped agencies migrate off monday.com to a custom portal. Both moves were the right call for the firm at the time. The decision is shape-driven, not preference-driven.

Most agencies and ops-driven SMBs should be on monday.com first. The custom-build conversation comes later, and the trigger is usually one of the signals under [How to decide](#how-to-decide) below.

## Where monday.com wins

Visual flexibility. The board view, timeline view, kanban, calendar are all real and well-built. For internal coordination, this is the best of the category.

Onboarding velocity. A team can be productive on monday.com in a week. Custom builds don't compete on this and shouldn't try.

Integrations. The marketplace is mature. Most SaaS tools you'd want to wire in have a sanctioned integration. The Slack and email integrations are solid.

Configurability without code. Custom fields, custom statuses, custom automations cover 80% of small-team needs. If your workflow fits inside that 80%, you should not be paying for a custom build.

[I rolled out monday.com](/work/monday-rollout-agency) for a creative agency across a 50-plus-user phased deployment with role-based boards, SOPs, automations, and dashboards. It worked. It still works. There's no shame in monday.com being the answer.

## Where monday.com breaks

Anyone with edit rights can change a status, and nothing checks whether the step before it happened. That is fine for your team. It is the wrong shape once a status sends an invoice, locks a deliverable or starts a contract.

The automations are fine for simple rules. Anything with several steps, or anything that has to recover when a step fails, breaks. Teams add Zapier or Make.com on top, and now you are maintaining a stack of tools that only half fit together.

The record of changes is thin. There is a change log, but it is not the kind a finance or compliance team will accept for contracts and money. If you have to prove who changed what, for a regulator or under a contract, monday.com is not where those records should live.

monday.com's AI summarizes columns and suggests automations. It does not do the work inside your process, like running a client sign-off or drafting a brief from a prospect's website. The whole category is behind here and will be for a while.

Past 30 seats, the per-seat price adds up fast. Add the Pro features, the higher automation limits and the marketplace integrations you actually use, and the yearly total ends up well above the price on the pricing page.

## What custom looks like as the next step

[The agency portal I built for a 4-year client](/work/bs-hub) is the working version of "monday.com plus everything monday.com couldn't model." Every inquiry moves through 12 fixed steps. Every deliverable needs sign-off before it moves on. Changes to contracts and money are kept for seven years. An AI intake asks new prospects follow-up questions and drafts a brand brief from their website, and a nightly check emails the admins when something looks wrong. The agency's own brand runs all the way through.

The useful part is that the pattern carries over to the next client, and it is a long way past a board. A recount on 21 July 2026 put it at 84 database models, 255 endpoints and 9 scheduled jobs, documented alongside the code. When the next agency outgrows monday.com, the build is "shape this to your delivery," not "invent everything from scratch." That is a 4 to 10 week build, not a 6 month one.

It also gets reviewed like production software rather than like a settings change. A review pass in July 2026 raised 63 findings on that codebase, and a second pass tried to disprove every one. Two rounds of fixes closed all 63, plus older issues pulled in along the way, 87 in total, and every test passed afterwards, 252 of 252. That is the sort of trail an automation tool cannot produce for you.

The point of going custom isn't to outdo monday.com on flexibility. It is to win the parts monday.com flattens: the steps, the record of changes, the AI inside the work, and your own brand in front of clients.

## How to decide

Run this in order:

1. **Is a status a note for your team, or does something depend on it?** If clients read it, finance invoices from it or a contract starts from it, something depends on it, and monday.com's statuses are too thin for that.
2. **Are your automations failing more often?** If yes, you've outgrown them. Adding Zapier or Make.com on top buys you 6 months. Going custom is the longer-term fix.
3. **Does your team need a written guide to use your monday.com setup?** If it does, the setup has outgrown the tool.
4. **Will you want AI doing real work inside the process in the next 12 months?** If yes, plan the custom path rather than waiting on monday.com's AI plans.
5. **Add up seats, Pro features and marketplace add-ons, and compare the total with a custom retainer.** It tips later than people expect, but it does tip.
